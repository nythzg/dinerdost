Priority gap checklist

P1

Recipe accuracy for sizes, add-ons and order channels

Store ingredient quantities for each size/add-on and optional takeaway/delivery packaging. Resolve and snapshot the selected recipe before reservation/consumption.

Current options change selling price, while reserveStock joins the base recipe by item_id. A configured small/large or extra-cheese option cannot have a distinct ingredient recipe. This is a capability gap verified in code, not a claim that every existing dish is misconfigured.

P1

Custom units, pack conversions and material yield

Support box/carton/bottle purchase units mapped to one base stock unit, plus explicit conversion factors and measured normal preparation loss.

kg/L/unit and fractional base quantities work today. They do not describe a carton of 24 bottles, multiple buying packs, peeled yield or material codes/categories/HSN metadata.

P1

Semi-finished goods and actual production

Add production recipes and batches that consume inputs, create sauce/batter/prep stock, inherit actual cost and record yield/loss. Link planned production to demand when required.

Our prep suggestion estimates dish demand; it does not create output inventory. A stockable sauce or batter requires a separate production lifecycle and cost trace.

P1

Complete purchase and receiving lifecycle

Add ordered line quantities/rates, delivery schedules, partial receipts, named approvers, supplier invoice totals and requested/dispatched/received comparisons. Add auditable purchase returns/debit-note handling.

Today a purchase is essentially vendor/reference/approved amount/note plus a one-time itemised receipt. The missing ordered and dispatch quantities prevent a meaningful three-way quantity check.

P1

Supplier payables and settlement

Track supplier invoices, due balances, partial/full payments, allocation across invoices, returns/credits and supplier statements.

Approving a purchase records an expense; it is not a supplier payment ledger. Customer payment records do not settle vendor invoices.

P1

Inventory reporting depth and financial variance

Add a unified movement bridge, valued physical shortages/surpluses, ingredient drill-down per order, item-wise purchase rates, supplier balances, period comparisons and complete exports.

Our count report already compares physical quantity with recorded recipe/receipt/waste movements. It does not yet freeze a money value for that discrepancy or provide every specialised drill-down shown in the tutorials.

P2

Bulk setup, templates and scanner operations

Provide validated material/recipe/vendor/count imports, downloadable count sheets, branch copy, material barcodes/labels, scanner input and procurement document/email templates.

Existing menu photo upload, Table QR and count CSV export solve different tasks. These tools would reduce onboarding and daily entry effort.

P2

Planning and waste operations

Calculate ingredient needs from planned dishes/servings, subtract usable stock and suggest a purchase request. Add dish-level wastage via recipes and affected-dish expiry views.

Same-weekday forecasting, raw-batch wastage and expiry alerts are already present; the ingredient indent and richer waste workflow are additional capabilities.

Chain phase

Inter-outlet and central-kitchen supply chain

Map kitchens and outlets; create linked requisition/dispatch/transit/receipt and return transactions; add rate cards, material trading/challans and route/outlet picking reports.

Having several outlets and role-scoped access does not create a balanced stock transfer. These features matter most for central kitchens, franchises and multi-outlet brands.

Optional / deferred

Supplier marketplace, offline host and bar-specific registers

Treat a supplier network as a separate ecosystem, offline reconciliation as a separate host/sync project, and excise output as a jurisdiction-specific bar requirement.

Do not replace real supplier/merchant/provider data with demo buttons. Offline/native/provider work remains deferred; applicability and current report rules need separate validation before implementation.

Preserve deliberate differences

FIFO versus moving average. The videos use configurable moving-average purchase costs. Diner Dost records FIFO batch costs and historical item-cost snapshots. This is a costing-policy difference; switching methods must not rewrite issued invoices or recorded costs.

Physical observations versus balance correction. Q45 records current-day physical observations separately from stock balances. Video 64 also shows past-date stock edits and automatic next-opening carry-forward. Do not silently adopt those behaviours or treat uncounted days as zero.

Reorder controls. The client explicitly requested removing the reorder UI. Threshold alerts, par levels and automatic suggested orders in the reference are benchmark differences to discuss, not an instruction to restore that UI.

Approval scope. Tutorial 65 is about supplier purchasing/prepayment approval. It does not justify restoring the removed cashier Verify payment step. Owner-only tax/charge access also stays intact.

Client recipe data. Recipes and purchase rates must be configured with actual client data. The client deferred recipe entry; unknown usage/cost must remain flagged. No report can establish theft from a stock difference alone.
